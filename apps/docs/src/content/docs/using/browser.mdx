Every bot has a browser of its own: a Chrome profile that belongs to that bot alone. Sign it in to
a site once and it stays signed in. Nothing is shared between bots, or with your own Chrome.

Inside that browser, each session (the conversation, every subagent, every run of an automation)
browses in a window of its own, so sessions running at once never get in each other's way. A
subagent's or a run's window closes a minute after it finishes.

## Watching

When a bot starts browsing, a small live view floats over its chat. From there you can:

- **dock it beside the chat** (the dock button), or make it fill the window;
- pop it out in **picture in picture**, to keep an eye on it while you do something else;
- close it; the bot goes on browsing.

**Settings → Browser → Show the browser when a bot starts browsing** decides whether the live view
opens by itself. The live view works the same from another computer or the phone. Separately, a
bot's **Show the browser window** setting also shows its real Chrome window on the computer that
runs it; off, Chrome runs without a window there and you watch it only in the app.

## Taking control

Watching is not driving: while you watch, nothing you do reaches the page.

<Steps>

1. Press **Take control**. The page is now yours to use, with the mouse and keyboard, from the
   computer or the phone. On a computer that is not the one in control, the button reads **Take
   control here**.

2. Do what is needed: sign in, solve a CAPTCHA, pick a seat. The address bar, back, forward,
   reload and tabs work as in any browser.

3. Press **Hand back**. The bot is told you used the window, and carries on from where the page is
   now.

</Steps>

While you have control, the bot can still read the page (`browser_snapshot`, `browser_read`), but
anything else it tries in that window is refused at once, with a note saying so. So you and the
bot never act on a page at the same time.

## When a bot asks for you

A bot that needs you for something it cannot or should not do (a sign-in, a CAPTCHA, a payment
form) asks with `browser_ask_user`. Its window shows what it needs ("Sign in to LinkedIn") with
**Take control** and **Not now**. The bot waits until you hand back, say no, or 25 minutes pass.
Runs of automations cannot ask, since nobody is watching them.

## Downloads and files

What a bot downloads lands in its `files/inbox/`, under a name of its own. The browser opens web
addresses only: `http`, `https`, and blank or data pages. Files on the computer stay out of it,
whoever is driving.

## Which browser

**Settings → Browser → Browser program** chooses what the bots browse with: Google Chrome, Microsoft
Edge, or the Chromium that comes with OpenKumi. The page also lists
which bots have a browser running now, with a way to stop each. `OPENKUMI_BROWSER_CHANNEL`
overrides the setting.

<Aside type="caution" title="A bot's browser holds real logins">
  Web pages can contain text written to manipulate an AI. Bots are told to treat what they read as
  material, never instructions, and to ask you before buying, sending or deleting anything, but
  that is a safeguard, not a guarantee. Sign a bot in only to what you are comfortable with it
  using. See [What a bot can reach](/security/bots/).
</Aside>
