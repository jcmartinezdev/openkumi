A bot is someone you talk to, not a list of chats. Each bot has **one conversation** with you:
whatever you say lands in the same place, and the bot can look up anything said there, however
long ago.

## Writing while it works

You never have to wait for a bot to finish. What you write while it is busy waits in a queue, in
view at the foot of the conversation (on the computer and the phone), and the bot reads it when the
turn it is on is over, one message at a time.

- **×** takes a waiting message back before the bot has read it.
- **Send now** has the bot read a waiting message as it works, ahead of the others, so you can
  change its course without stopping it ("actually, only direct flights").
- **Stop** stops the bot. It does not then start again by itself on what was waiting: those
  messages are held back until you write to it, press **Resume**, or send one of them now.
- At most ten messages wait for a bot. One more is refused, and you keep what you wrote.

[The queue](/how-it-works/queue/) explains how each engine takes a message mid-turn.

## Sending files

Attach files to a message with **Attach files** in the composer, or drop or paste them there: up
to ten at once,
100 MB each. The conversation keeps a copy, which you can open from the message later. When the bot
reads the message, it gets a copy of its own in `files/inbox/`, which it can keep, move or tidy
away like any of its files.

Every engine looks at pictures you send (PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP); Claude Code also reads PDFs. Any
other file reaches the bot as a file to open with its tools. A file taken out of a message before
it was sent is thrown away.

## Talking instead of typing

The microphone in the composer, on the computer and on the phone, writes what you say into the
message as you say it, where the cursor was. It is written down on the device itself, with Apple's
speech models (macOS 26 or iOS 26 and later), so no sound leaves it, and the words go to the bot
only when you send them, like anything typed. **Esc** (or **×**) gives up and leaves the message
as it was; **Enter** while it listens sends once the last words are in.

To talk out loud and hear the bot answer, [call it](/calls/).

## What you see while it works

- **Steps.** Each thing the bot does (a page opened, a file written, a search) shows as a step.
  Once a turn is over, its steps fold into one line ("12 steps · 1m 04s"). **Settings → Chat →
  Show the details of each step** chooses how much a step shows.
- **Its browser.** When the bot starts browsing, a small live view of its browser floats over the
  chat. See [A bot's browser](/using/browser/).
- **Subagents.** A bot can split work into parallel subagents, each in a thread of its own beneath
  the conversation. Watch them live, open one to read or write to it, or stop it. What each comes
  to arrives back in the conversation as a report. See [Subagents](/how-it-works/subagents/).
- **Messages between bots.** A bot passing work to another shows it in both conversations ("To
  Travel", "From Kumi"); either opens the other end.
- **Automation results** arrive as cards, with the bot's word on them.

## What a reply can hold

Replies are drawn the same way on the computer and the phone: code in colour with a copy button,
tables that scroll sideways, ```` ```mermaid ```` diagrams, and formulas (`$…$` within a line,
`$$…$$` on lines of their own). When a picture says more than words, a bot can show a page in the
conversation (a chart, a dashboard, a timeline) that follows light and dark mode and opens full
size. See [Replies and pages](/how-it-works/replies-and-pages/).

## When a bot has something for you

Much of what a bot does happens while you are elsewhere: an automation ran, another bot wrote to
it, a subagent finished. So each bot wears one mark beside its name, in the sidebar on the
computer and in the list on the phone. The first of these that holds is the one it wears:

| Mark | What it means | When it goes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Needs you** | The bot waits for you: for a step in its browser, with messages held back after it was stopped, or because it asked you something or said it cannot go on without your answer. | When you do the step, let the messages go, answer, or write to it. |
| **Read this** | The bot told you something that should not wait until you next look, such as a meeting moved to this afternoon. | When you open its conversation. |
| A spinner | It is at work. | When it is done. |
| A red dot | Its last run failed. | When it next runs. |
| A blue dot, before the time | It said something you have not read. | When you open its conversation. |

What you have read is kept by the environment, not by the device: read a conversation on the
phone and its mark goes on the computer too. A conversation counts as read while it is in front of
you, in a window or an app that is in front.

**Needs you** and **Read this** come with the reason in the bot's own few words: under its name
on the phone, and on the computer when the pointer rests on its row. When a bot asked for your
answer, the reason also stands over the message box in its conversation, with **Clear** to take
the mark away without answering. Reading alone does not: the bot is still waiting.

### When it asks you to choose

When what a bot needs is a choice (which of two flights, whether to go ahead, a detail only you
know), it asks with a card over the message box instead of a paragraph to read. Each question
comes with the answers the bot thinks likely, the one it would recommend first:

- Press an answer to choose it. Where several may fit, the card says so and each is ticked by
  itself.
- **Something else…** takes an answer in your own words, in place of the options or, where several
  may fit, beside them.
- With more than one question the card shows one at a time: choosing an answer goes on to the
  next, **Back** returns, and **Skip** leaves one out.
- **Send answer** sends what you chose to the bot as a message from you, which you can read back
  in the conversation, and the bot goes on from there.

The card is the same on the computer and the phone, and answering on one closes it on the other.
You can also ignore the options and write to the bot as usual, or close the card with **×**: that
takes **Needs you** away without answering. The questions stay in the conversation, where the bot
asked them.

It is the bot that decides to raise either of the two, and it is told to keep them for what cannot
wait: a daily summary is a blue dot, not a notification. When a bot raises too much or too little,
tell it so; it writes that into its own instructions.

On the computer, the app's icon counts the bots with **Needs you**, **Read this** or a blue dot,
and a notification is shown for the first two while OpenKumi is not in front. Clicking it opens
the bot. On a Mac both go on after you close the window, for as long as OpenKumi is running: the
window comes back from the Dock. **Settings → General → Notify me when a bot needs me** turns the
notifications off for that computer; the count stays. The phone shows the marks whenever the app
is open, and has no notifications of its own yet.

## Starting fresh, and earlier conversations

**Start fresh**, in the conversation's header, sets the conversation so far aside and begins an
empty one. What the bot knows is untouched, because that lives in its folder, not in the
conversation. The clock button beside it lists earlier conversations and searches everything that
was said; any of them can be read, or continued in place of the current one.

Nothing is deleted unless you delete it: every message, run and subagent thread is kept.
**Settings → Advanced → Delete all conversations** is there when you want a clean slate.

<Aside title="Long conversations">
  A conversation that never ends cannot be sent to a model whole for ever. Engines shorten it by
  themselves; with a local model or an API, OpenKumi summarises the older part. You still see every
  message, and the bot can look up anything said with `search_history` and `read_history`. See
  [One conversation per bot](/how-it-works/conversation/).
</Aside>
