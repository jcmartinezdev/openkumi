A call is a conversation with a bot out loud: you talk, it talks back at once, and you can
interrupt it as you would a person. The phone button at the top of a bot's conversation starts
one, in the desktop app and in the iPhone app.

On the computer, a small panel stays over the window while the call lasts, with who is on the
line, what is being said, a way to mute your microphone and a way to hang up; the call goes on
while you look at another bot or at the settings. The computer needs a microphone.

## On the iPhone

The call comes up as a screen of its own: the bot, how long you have been talking, what is being
said, and three buttons, **Mute**, **Speaker** and **Hang up**. Pull the screen down, or press
the arrow at its top, and the call goes on while you read the conversation or do something else
in the app. A bar under the title then says who is on the line and for how long; its red button
hangs up, and a tap on the rest of it brings the call's screen back.

- A call starts on the loudspeaker. **Speaker** puts it at your ear instead, like a phone call,
  and the screen goes dark while the phone is held there. When headphones or a car are
  connected, the sound is theirs.
- The call goes on while the phone is locked or another app is in front.
- The first call asks whether OpenKumi may use the microphone. If that was refused, the phone's
  Settings are where to allow it; the app offers to open them.
- While a call is on, the microphone in the composer (dictation) is put away: the microphone is
  the call's.
- A call made on the computer shows on the phone as well, and can be ended from there, and the
  other way round.

The phone needs nothing but the app. What a call needs (below) is needed on the computer that
runs your bots, as for a call made there.

## What a call needs

**Codex, signed in with a ChatGPT plan**, on the computer that runs OpenKumi. The voice on a call
is a speech model that comes with the plan, and Codex is the program that reaches it. There is no
API key, and nothing more to pay for or set up: if a bot can run on Codex there, a bot can be
called there.

That holds whatever the bot itself runs on. A bot on Claude Code, Grok or a local model is called
the same way: the voice is ChatGPT's, the work is still done by the bot's own engine.

## Setting it up

<Steps>

1. On the computer that runs the bots, install Codex and run `codex login`, signing in with your
   ChatGPT account (not an API key).

2. In **Settings → Providers**, check that Codex shows as signed in with ChatGPT and is switched
   on.

3. Optionally, pick the bot's **Voice**, under **Calls** in its settings. A bot that was not given
   one has the usual voice. Each voice can be [heard first](#hearing-a-voice-before-choosing-it).

4. Press the phone button at the top of the bot's conversation. The first time, your computer or
   phone asks whether OpenKumi may use the microphone.

</Steps>

When calls are not available, the phone button says what is missing: Codex is not installed, is
not signed in, is signed in with an API key instead of a plan, or is switched off in Settings.

The way Codex reaches the speech model is one OpenAI marks experimental. A new version of Codex
may change it; if calls stop working after Codex was updated, update OpenKumi.

## Who is on the line

The voice speaks as the bot, by name, and knows what the two of you were last talking about.
Asked who it is, it says so: the bot, in OpenKumi, who made OpenKumi, and the model the bot is
powered by, as the bot does in writing. Asked what is behind the voice, it says that the voice
comes with your ChatGPT plan.
Small talk, thinking out loud and anything it can answer from the call, it answers itself, at
once.

Everything else it hands to the bot: anything about your own things (your calendar, mail, files,
notes, accounts), anything that needs the web, anything to be done. What it hands over arrives in
the bot's conversation as a message from you, marked **On a call**, and the bot answers it on its
own engine. A bot that is at work on something else gets to it right after, and the voice says so.

### What a bot can do on a call

Only pass the work on. In the turn that answers something asked on a call, the bot has none of
its usual tools (no files, no memory, no browser, no web search, no superpowers), only these:

- **Other bots.** It can see who the other bots are and write to one (`list_bots`,
  `message_bot`); the main assistant can also make a new bot or change one (`create_bot`,
  `update_bot`).
- **Background tasks.** It can start a subagent (`spawn_subagent`), and look at, write to or stop
  the ones it started. A subagent is the bot itself at work in the background, in a thread of its
  own, with all the bot's tools: that is where something is looked up, a file is written, or
  something is remembered. It cannot wait for one; the result comes back by itself.
- **Automations.** It can make, change and delete automations that start at a time, for what is
  to happen later or again ("remind me at five"). One that starts on a watch or when an app calls
  is a subagent's to set up, because setting it up looks at a page or an app there and then.

So the bot's first answer on a call is usually that something is under way, and the voice says
that. When the work comes back to the conversation (the subagent's report, the other bot's reply,
the automation's result), the bot answers there as it always does, with all its tools again, and
the voice reads that out too, for as long as the call lasts. The panel says **Working on it…**
meanwhile, and you can go on talking. Only what was started on the call is read out: something
else that happens to come in meanwhile, from work you asked for in writing or from another bot,
stays in the conversation and is not sent to the speech model.

There is one way round this, and it is yours: when a request from a call is still waiting for a
bot that is at work on something else, pressing **Send now** on it puts it into the turn that is
already running, which has all the bot's tools.

This keeps the call free, and it means nothing is done on a few spoken words alone: what you
asked for, what was started for it and what came of it are all in the conversation, to read
afterwards. It also costs time. Something the bot would look up in a few seconds when you write
to it takes fifteen or more on a call, because a subagent has to be started and report back. A
bot's reply to something asked on a call is written to be said aloud: short, the answer first.
When there is more than can be said (a table, a document, a page), the bot puts it in the
conversation and says that it is there.

When the call ends, the conversation gets a record of it: how long it lasted, and everything that
was said, to unfold.

### When the voice keeps something to itself

What to hand over is the voice's own choice, and it does not always choose right: it may say
"I'll remember that" and hand nothing over. Two things catch that. Every request the bot gets
from a call comes with what was said just before it, and the bot is told to do what was asked or
promised there too. And when a call ends, the bot itself goes over what you said after the last
thing the voice handed over, in the background (a subagent called **After the call**), and does
what it finds: keeps what was to be remembered, sets up what was to be scheduled. It is told what
was handed over during the call, so that nothing is done twice, and it leaves anything that needs
your go-ahead for you. You hear of it in the conversation only when there was something to do. A
call on which you said nothing more after the last thing handed over is not gone over at all.

## Hearing a voice before choosing it

A voice can be heard before it is chosen. It says one short line as the bot ("Hi, I'm Kumi. This
is how I sound when you call me."), over a line like a call's, so it sounds the way it will on a
call; it takes a couple of seconds to come on.

The voices are sorted **Female**, **Male** and **Languages**, and each says what it is like: the
nine that came first in OpenAI's own words ("Composed and direct"), the newer ones, each made for a
language, by that language. OpenAI does not say which voices are female or male; the app goes by
how they are commonly known. Codex also lists Marin, Porto and Selva, which OpenAI does not put on
the line, so they are left out.

- **On the computer**, the play button beside the bot's voice says its line. The voice itself opens
  a page with all of them: click one and it says its line, or go through them with the arrow keys.
  **Use …**, or Enter, makes the voice in view the bot's.
- **On the iPhone**, Voice opens a page with the voices side by side, one in view at a time, and a
  switch above them for each kind. Swipe to the next one and it says its line by itself; the play
  button says it again. **Use …** makes the voice in view the bot's.

Hearing a voice is a very short call on your ChatGPT plan, with nobody listening: the speech
model only speaks to a line that sends it sound, so the app opens the microphone and keeps it
switched off, and what it sends is silence. That is why the first time asks for the microphone.
No voice can be heard while a call is on.

## Where the sound goes

Between the device you call from (the computer or the phone) and OpenAI, directly. The app makes
one half of the connection and the OpenKumi server passes it to Codex, which sets the call up
with your ChatGPT sign-in and hands back the other half. The server never carries the sound, and
no recording is kept anywhere by OpenKumi: only the words, as the speech model wrote them down.

What OpenAI receives is what a call is made of: your voice; the bot's name and the line saying what
it is for; today's date and time zone; the last eight messages of your conversation with it, each
cut short (so that the voice knows what you were talking about); and the bot's answers to what you
asked on the call. The bot's own instructions, memory and files are not sent; they stay with the
bot. [How a call works](/how-it-works/calls/) goes through it step by step.

## When a call ends by itself

- Nothing was said for five minutes, and the bot is not at work on anything you asked. A call
  left open would only use up your plan.
- The app that made it went away: it was closed, or the server has not heard from it for 40
  seconds.
- Another call was started. There is one call at a time.
- The bot was deleted.
- Your plan does not allow more voice use for now. The panel, or the bar on the phone, says what
  Codex said.

What the bot was asked to do before the call ended, it still does: the answer is in the
conversation.
