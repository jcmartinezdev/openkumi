A call has two paths. **The sound** goes straight between the app you call from (the desktop app
or the iPhone app) and OpenAI's speech model, over WebRTC. **The words** that matter (what the
voice hands over, and what the bot answers) go through Codex and the OpenKumi server. The server never carries sound. For how a call feels in
use, see [Calls](/calls/).

## Setting it up

1. The app opens the microphone (with echo cancellation), makes its half of a WebRTC connection,
   and sends it to the server with the bot to call.
2. The server starts Codex's app server in an empty folder (`calls/workspace` in the data folder), with
   everything that could run commands or touch files switched off, and asks it to start a voice
   call with the app's half, in the bot's voice.
3. Codex sets the call up with OpenAI on your ChatGPT sign-in, and hands back OpenAI's half, which
   the server passes to the app. From then on the app and OpenAI talk directly. The whole set-up
   has 25 seconds to finish.

There is one call at a time; starting another ends the first.

## What the voice is told

The voice is not the bot: it is a speech model, told to speak as the bot and to hand over almost
everything. It is given:

- the bot's name, whether it is the main assistant, and the line saying what it is for;
- rules for what to answer itself and what to hand over, with examples;
- today's date and the time zone;
- the last eight messages of the conversation, each cut to 700 characters, of which only what you
  wrote and what the bot answered (not what other bots or tools put in).

It is not given the bot's rules, instructions, notes or files.

## A request, handed over

When the voice hands something over, Codex passes it to the server, which puts it in the bot's
queue as a message from you, marked **On a call**, with what was said just before it. The bot
answers it in a turn of its own, on its own engine, with only the tools for passing work on (see
[What a bot can do on a call](/calls/#what-a-bot-can-do-on-a-call)). Its answer goes back through
Codex for the voice to say, cut at 3,500 characters.

Results of work started on the call (a subagent's report, another bot's reply, an automation's
result) come back into the conversation later. The bot answers them there with all its tools, and
that answer is read out too, while the call lasts. Nothing else that reaches the conversation
meanwhile is said or sent to the speech model.

## Ending

The app tells the server it is still there every ten seconds. The server ends the call after five
minutes in which nothing was said and the bot is not at work on anything asked, when it has not
heard from the app for 40 seconds, when the bot is deleted, or when the app hangs up.

When the call ends, the conversation gets its record (how long, and what was said). If you said
something after the last thing the voice handed over, the bot goes over it in a subagent called
**After the call**, and does what was asked or promised: keeps what was to be remembered, sets up
what was to be scheduled, and leaves anything that needs your go-ahead. If it finds nothing to do,
you do not hear of it.

<Aside>
  The way Codex reaches the speech model is one OpenAI marks experimental. A new version of Codex
  may change it; if calls stop working after Codex was updated, update OpenKumi.
</Aside>
