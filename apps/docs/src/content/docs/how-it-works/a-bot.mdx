A bot is three things: **a folder** that holds what it is and what it knows, **a conversation**
with you, and **turns**, in which a model reads what the bot is told and the conversation, and acts
with the bot's tools.

## What a bot is made of

A bot is a folder in `~/.openkumi/bots/`. The folder is split by who it belongs to, and the split
is what keeps a bot from granting itself anything.

<BotAnatomy caption="What a bot can do is yours: its model, rules and superpowers sit outside its reach. What it knows is its own: it rewrites its instructions and notes freely, and every change is recorded. Its sign-ins are kept where it can never see them." />

- **Yours** (`bot.md`, `superpowers.json`): its name, what it is for, the model it runs on, your
  rules, and the superpowers you switched on. The bot is told what they say, but its tools cannot
  reach them.
- **The bot's own** (`workspace/`): `AGENTS.md`, its instructions to itself; `memory/`, its notes;
  `files/`, its work and what it is sent. Its file tools reach this folder and nothing else.
- **Private** (`private/`): its browser profile, its accounts with the apps of its superpowers,
  the history of changes to its workspace, and the files sent in its conversation. Never visible to
  the bot.

[Where everything lives](/how-it-works/where-everything-lives/) lists every file.

## The life of one message

Here is what happens between the moment you send a message and the bot's reply, for a bot on an
engine (Codex, Claude Code or Grok).

1. **Your message goes into the bot's queue.** Everything said to a bot waits there first: what you
   write, what another bot writes, and what the bot's own subagents and automations report. One
   message goes in at a time. See [The queue](/how-it-works/queue/).
2. **The turn starts.** The server puts together what the bot is told: the master prompt, filled in
   with who the other bots are, your rules, its own `AGENTS.md`, the list of its notes and its
   superpowers. See [What every bot is told](/how-it-works/what-a-bot-is-told/).
3. **The engine is started** for this turn, with its own tools for running commands and touching
   files switched off and your personal setup left out. It is given the bot's tools instead, at an
   address that exists only for this turn. When the engine already knows the conversation from an
   earlier turn, it is only sent what is new.
4. **The model works.** The engine sends the prompt to your plan with your own sign-in, and the
   model answers with text or a tool to call.
5. **Tools run in the server.** Each tool call comes back to the server, which runs it with the
   bot's workspace, browser or accounts, records it in the conversation, and sends the result
   back. The bot can use many tools in a turn; each one shows as a step in the app.
6. **The reply streams** to every app connected, word by word, as it is written.
7. **The turn ends.** The tools address stops working, and the next message in the queue goes in.
   When there is none, the bot is idle until something new arrives.

A bot on a local model or an API works the same way, except that the server runs the loop itself,
asking the model for one step at a time. See [Engines and models](/how-it-works/engines/).

## Turns that are not yours

Not every turn starts with a message from you:

- **A subagent** is the bot itself, at work on a part of a job in a thread of its own; its result
  comes back into the conversation as a message. See [Subagents](/how-it-works/subagents/).
- **An automation's run** is a thread of its own too, started at a time, by a watch or by an app's
  call; its result comes back the same way. See [Automations](/automations/).
- **Another bot** can write into the conversation, to pass work on or ask for something. See
  [Messages between bots](/how-it-works/messages-between-bots/).

In each case the bot knows where the message came from, and is told that only you can give it
the go-ahead to buy, send or delete something.

## What stays between turns

- **In the folder:** its instructions (`AGENTS.md`), its notes, its files, and your rules. This is
  what a bot knows, and it is the same whatever engine it runs on.
- **In the conversation:** every message and step, kept in the database. The bot can search and
  read it with `search_history` and `read_history`.
- **In its browser:** its sign-ins and cookies, in its own Chrome profile.
- **In the engine:** its own record of the conversation, so a turn only needs what is new. A bot
  moved to another engine is told what was said most recently instead.

<Aside title="One bot, many threads at once">
  A bot can be at work in several threads at once (its conversation, a few subagents, an
  automation's run), each with its own turns, its own browser window and its own queue. They share
  the bot's folder, sign-ins and superpowers.
</Aside>
