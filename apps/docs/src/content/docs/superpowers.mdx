A superpower is something a bot can do beyond chatting, browsing and keeping its files: working in
one of your apps, or know-how for a kind of job. You switch each one on for one bot at a time, and
a bot can use what you gave it but cannot change it or give itself more.

## What there is

Every superpower ships with OpenKumi; there is no way to add one from the app. They are grouped by
what they are for (Productivity, Work, Creative, Marketing, Travel, Everyday), and the same one can
be in several groups.

**Apps**, which a bot signs in to with an account of its own:

| For | Superpowers |
| --- | --- |
| Tasks and notes | Notion, Todoist, ClickUp, Evernote, Readwise |
| Work | Linear, Atlassian (Jira and Confluence), Miro, Sentry, PostHog |
| Customers and documents | Attio, folk, PandaDoc, Jotform |
| Meetings | Granola, Otter.ai, Fathom, tl;dv |
| Sites and marketing | Webflow, Wix, Klaviyo, Typefully |
| Money | PocketSmith |

**Know-how**, with nothing to sign in to: Weather, Exchange rates, Holidays, Watch a page, SEO &
AI search, Social posts, Launch, Landing pages, Competitors. These use free services the bot
reaches with `web_fetch`, or show it how to use its own browser, files, pages and automations for a
kind of job.

Each superpower has a page in the app that says what it does and lets you read every file it gives
a bot.

## Switching one on

<Steps>

1. Open **Settings → Superpowers** and pick one, or open a bot's settings and go to its
   superpowers.

2. Choose **Activate superpower** for the bot you want. Nothing is switched on for all bots at
   once.

3. If it works in an app, sign the bot in (below). It is active once the bot is signed in.

4. Ask the bot to use it. It is told one line about each of its superpowers and where its
   instructions are, and reads them when a task calls for it.

</Steps>

Deactivating a superpower for a bot forgets that bot's sign-in. A bot whose sign-in lapsed shows
**Sign in again**.

## Signing a bot in to an app

**Activate superpower** on one that works in an app opens a popup that asks how:

- **With your browser** (the desktop app, installed). The app's own sign-in page opens in the
  browser on your computer, with the accounts you already have there. It sends the browser back to
  `openkumi://sign-in/callback`, which your computer opens with OpenKumi, and the app passes that
  on to the environment it shows. This works whichever computer the bots run on.
- **On the phone.** The sign-in page opens in a sheet that has the accounts you have in Safari,
  and the sheet hands the way back to the app, which passes it on the same way.
- **Inside the bot's browser.** The page opens in a window of the bot's own browser, which you use
  from the app as you would any window you take control of. It suits giving a bot an account other
  than yours. This also leaves the bot's browser signed in to that app's website; the other two
  ways do not.

Either way the bot gets an account of its own (two bots can use two different accounts), and
cancelling leaves the superpower as it was, with nothing kept.

## What a superpower is made of

Each superpower is a folder in the code, `apps/server/superpowers/<id>`:

```
superpower.json   name, tagline, groups, what a bot can do with it, the line the bot is told,
                  and its service (an MCP server), when it has one
icon.svg          its icon
README.md         its page in the app
instructions.md   how the bot goes about it; the bot reads it when a task calls for it
```

Any other file in the folder is the bot's to read too, and you can read every one of them (never
change them) on the superpower's page. A superpower has instructions, a service, or both. The app
ones are both: instructions, and the app's own MCP server, whose tools the bot gets as
`mcp__<superpower>__<tool>`. The know-how ones are instructions only.

`superpower.json` can also say which of a service's tools only read (`mcp.reads`, so a
[watch](/automations/#what-starts-one) can call them) and how its app calls OpenKumi back
(`hooks`, for [webhooks](/webhooks/)).

<Aside type="caution" title="A superpower acts as the bot">
  A superpower's instructions are followed with the bot's own browser and accounts, so each is
  written to ask you before anything is sent, changed or paid. That is an instruction to the bot,
  not a lock: give a bot only the accounts you are comfortable with it using. See [What a bot can
  reach](/security/bots/).
</Aside>
