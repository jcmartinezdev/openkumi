OpenKumi has no server of its own in between: no account and no cloud. What leaves the computer
that runs your bots goes to services you chose, on your accounts or the bots' own. One thing goes
to OpenKumi's makers, and only if you agree when it asks: how OpenKumi is used and where it
breaks, never what you or your bots write.

<DataLeaves caption="What goes where. A bot on a local model sends nothing to an AI plan at all." />

| Where | What goes there | On whose account |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Your AI plan**: Anthropic, OpenAI or xAI, through the engine | what the bot is told (the master prompt, your rules, its `AGENTS.md`, the list of its notes, its superpowers), the conversation, what its tools found, and pictures or PDFs you sent it | yours, signed in to the program |
| **An API** you set up | the same | the key you gave |
| **A local model** | the same, but it never leaves the computer | — |
| **A superpower's app** | what the bot reads and does there | the bot's own sign-in |

| **GitHub** | a look for a newer version (the app every 4 hours, a server alone every 6), and the download | — |

What never leaves: your keys, the bots' tokens, their browser profiles, and the files in their
workspaces (unless a bot itself sends one somewhere with its tools). Dictation writes down what you
say on the device; only the words go to the bot, when you send them.

## Signing a bot in to an app

A bot signs in to a superpower's app (Notion, Linear and the rest) with an account of its own, by
the app's standard sign-in (OAuth). The steps are drawn on [Superpowers](/superpowers/#signing-a-bot-in-to-an-app).

- **It finishes only the way it was started.** Each sign-in has a one-time value the server keeps
  for 15 minutes. The way back has to carry it, for the same bot, by the same way it was begun
  (your browser, the phone, or the bot's browser), and only once.
- **It cannot be finished from outside.** The server has no address on the network that a sign-in
  can come back to. The way back from your own browser comes through the app, with its key; in the
  bot's browser, it is caught only from the window the sign-in opened, and OpenKumi's own address,
  where it points, cannot be reached from a bot's browser at all.
- **The tokens stay where the bot runs**, in its `private/` folder, readable only by your account,
  and are used by the server on the bot's behalf. The bot never sees them.
- **Deactivating** the superpower for that bot forgets the sign-in.

## Webhooks

<WebhookPath caption="Calls come in through your tunnel to a door of their own, on this computer only. A call is taken only when signed with the secret, once; the run is told ids, never text." />

- The door is a port of its own (4318), open on this computer only, that takes `POST /hooks/…` and
  nothing else, and only once a webhook is set up. The internet reaches it only through a tunnel
  you point at it.
- Each webhook has an address made of random letters nobody can guess, and a secret the app signs
  each call with (HMAC-SHA256). An unsigned or wrongly signed call gets the same answer as an
  address nobody set up. The same call is taken once.
- A run is told the kind of event, its ids and numbers, and which fields changed. Nothing anyone
  wrote in the app reaches it through the webhook; the bot reads the item with its own sign-in.

See [Webhooks](/webhooks/) for setting one up.

## Calls

The sound of a call goes straight between your computer and OpenAI; the OpenKumi server never
carries it, and no recording is kept. Only the words are kept in the conversation, as the speech
model wrote them down. See [How a call works](/how-it-works/calls/).

## Watches

A watch on a web page looks at public `https` pages only: addresses on this computer, the home
network or Tailscale are refused, also when a redirect or a name leads there, and what comes back is
read up to 1 MB. A watch on an app calls one of the bot's superpower tools that only reads.

<Aside>
  The engines are your own programs, and keep their own records of the conversations they ran, in
  their own folders (such as `~/.claude` and `~/.codex`), under their makers' terms.
</Aside>
