OpenKumi runs bots on your computer that hold real sign-ins: a browser logged in to your accounts,
apps they can write to, files they keep. So it is built round two questions. **Who may use the
server?** Only you, from devices you paired. **What may a bot do?** Only what you gave it, with
its own tools, and nothing it reads counts as your word.

<TrustMap caption="You are the only one trusted. The server checks every key and keeps each bot to its own tools. The bots act for you and can be misled, so whatever they read (pages, mail, other bots, files, webhook calls) is material, never an instruction." />

## The walls, in short

**Getting in** ([Keys, pairing and the network](/security/access/))

- Every request to the server carries a key. Nothing is trusted for where it comes from, and there
  are no cookies, so a web page open in a browser has no way in.
- The server answers only this computer and your home network, over plain `http`. A connection
  straight from Tailscale or anywhere else on the internet is closed before anything is read from
  it: from there, devices come through an `https` address you put in front of it.
- Pairing never sends its code over the network, and a device makes the server prove who it is
  before it sends its key to any address.
- Only the computer that runs the bots can let a new device in, or shut one out.
- Each person with bots on a computer has a server of their own, with keys of its own: a device
  paired with one person's server gets nothing from another's.

**What a bot can do** ([What a bot can reach](/security/bots/))

- A bot's file tools reach its own workspace and nothing else. Its rules, its superpowers and its
  sign-ins sit outside it, so it cannot grant itself anything.
- There is no tool for running commands. Claude Code, Codex and Grok run with their own tools for
  commands and files switched off.
- A bot holds no key to the server, and the address its engine reaches its tools at works for one
  turn, from this computer only.
- A bot uses the API keys and passwords in its [vault](/secrets/) by name, without seeing them.
  They are kept encrypted, and each is sent only to the sites it is for.
- Bots are told that pages, files, tool results and other bots' messages are material, never
  instructions, and to ask you before buying, sending or deleting anything.
- Pages a bot shows run in a frame of their own that cannot reach the app or its keys.

**What leaves your computer** ([What leaves your computer](/security/outside/))

- There is no OpenKumi server in between. Your conversations go to the AI plan the bot runs on,
  and nowhere else of ours.
- OpenKumi asks before it tells its makers anything, and then only how it is used and where it
  breaks ([Usage and error reports](/telemetry/)).
- A bot signs in to an app with an account of its own. The sign-in finishes only the way it was
  started, and its tokens never leave the computer that runs it.
- Webhooks come in through a door of their own, are taken only when signed, and pass the bot ids,
  never text anyone wrote.

<Aside type="caution" title="What this does not do">
  Instructions to a bot are not locks. A bot that reads a page written to mislead it can still be
  talked into something within what its tools and sign-ins allow; the rules and confirmations make
  that less likely, not impossible. [Known limits](/security/limits/) lists this and the other gaps
  plainly.
</Aside>

## Good habits

- **Give a bot only the sign-ins you are comfortable with it using.** A bot with your bank in its
  browser can be asked, or tricked, into using it. Separate bots for separate kinds of work keep
  each one's reach small.
- **Give a bot a key in its vault, never in a message.** A message is read by the AI service the
  bot runs on; a key typed into the bot's card, or under its **Secrets**, is not.
- **Put what must hold in its rules, not in a conversation.** Rules in `bot.md` are out of the
  bot's reach; its own instructions are not. Read **Workspace → Changes** now and then, and undo
  what you did not want.
- **Pair over networks you trust**, and away from home use an `https` address on Tailscale rather
  than opening a port to the internet. Remove devices you no longer use.
- **Keep your computer account to yourself.** There are no user accounts in OpenKumi: whoever can
  use your account on the computer that runs it can use your bots. Someone else in the house who
  has bots on the same computer is kept apart in the apps, but not from that account (see
  [Known limits](/security/limits/#your-computer-account-is-the-boundary)).
- **Keep OpenKumi up to date.** It updates itself unless you switched that off.
