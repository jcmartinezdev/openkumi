A webhook lets an app call OpenKumi the moment something happens there ("an issue was assigned"),
so an automation starts at once instead of at a [watch](/automations/#a-watch)'s next look. Linear
can, for now: a superpower says whether its app can, and how, in its `superpower.json` under
`hooks`.

Calls come from the internet, which OpenKumi otherwise refuses. So webhooks take a little setting
up, once, and a door of their own.

<WebhookPath caption="Linear calls your tunnel, which passes the call to a door that is open on this computer only. A call is taken only when it was signed with the secret; the run is told what happened by id, and the bot reads the rest with its own sign-in." />

## Before you start

- The bot has the **Linear** superpower, signed in.
- The computer that runs the bots is awake when Linear calls. Linear tries again after a minute,
  an hour and six hours, then gives up and may switch the webhook off. For what must not be
  missed, a [watch](/automations/#a-watch) is the safer choice.
- You can make a tunnel from the internet to that computer: [Tailscale
  Funnel](https://tailscale.com/kb/1223/funnel) is the easiest, or any tunnel or proxy of your own
  that serves `https`.

## Setting it up

<Steps>

1. **Open the door to the internet.** Webhooks are taken on a port of their own: the server's port
   plus one, `4318` unless `OPENKUMI_HOOKS_PORT` says otherwise (Linear's page under
   **Settings → Superpowers** says which). It listens on this computer only, and takes
   `POST /hooks/…` and nothing else. Point a tunnel at it, for example:

   ```sh
   tailscale funnel 4318
   ```

2. **Give OpenKumi the public address.** On Linear's page under **Settings → Superpowers**, enter
   the address your tunnel has under **Public address**. OpenKumi suggests this computer's
   Tailscale name (`https://my-mac.example.ts.net`); any `https` address works. One address serves
   every webhook.

3. **Set up the bot's webhook.** On the same page, choose **Set up webhook** beside the bot.
   OpenKumi gives it an address nobody can guess, and shows the steps for Linear:

   - In Linear, open **Settings → API**, and under **Webhooks** choose **New webhook**. Only an
     admin of the workspace can.
   - Give it a name, and enter the address OpenKumi shows as its URL.
   - Under **Data change events** tick **Issues** and **Comments**, and choose the team it is for,
     or all public teams.
   - Create it, open it, copy its **signing secret**, and give it to OpenKumi.

4. **Make an automation that starts on it.** In the automation form, **Starts** offers "When
   Linear calls" for a bot whose webhook is set up, with what to run on (an issue is created,
   changed or removed; a comment is written). Or ask the bot: it makes one with
   `create_automation` and `hook`, though it cannot set a webhook up itself.

</Steps>

## What is checked, and what is passed on

- **A call is taken only when Linear signed it** with the secret (an HMAC of the body, in the
  `Linear-Signature` header). Any other call gets the same answer as an address nobody set up.
- **The same call is taken once.** A call that comes again is recognised and dropped.
- **The secret stays on the server.** It is kept in OpenKumi's database and never sent anywhere.
- **A run is told what happened by id alone**: the kind of event, the ids and numbers in it, and
  for a change the names of the fields that changed. Nothing anyone wrote in Linear is passed on
  (no titles, no comments, no descriptions), so text in an issue cannot reach the bot through the
  webhook. The bot reads the issue itself, with its own sign-in, as it would anything else there,
  and treats what it reads as material, not instructions.
- **Calls are gathered.** Calls that come within five minutes of a run wait and are answered
  together in the next one, up to 50 at a time. While an automation is paused, its calls are kept,
  and it is given them when it is turned on again.

## Bots for someone else on the same computer

Each person with bots on a computer ([Bots for someone else in the
house](/environments/#bots-for-someone-else-in-the-house)) has a server of their own, and so a
door of their own: their server's port plus one, `4328` for the second person, `4338` for the
third. Their public address, their webhooks and their secrets are their own, set up from their
app, and a call that comes in at one person's door never reaches another person's bots.

So each needs a tunnel of their own, pointed at their own door. Tailscale Funnel opens only the
ports 443, 8443 and 10000 to the internet, and `tailscale funnel 4318` above gave 443 to the
first person's. Give the second person another of them:

```sh
tailscale funnel --https=8443 4328
```

Their public address is then the computer's Tailscale name with that port,
`https://my-mac.example.ts.net:8443`. Type it in under **Public address**: the address OpenKumi
suggests there has no port.

## Taking it down

**Remove webhook** on Linear's page stops its address working at once and pauses the automations
that start on its calls. Deactivating the superpower for that bot does the same. Remove the webhook
in Linear too.

<Aside type="caution">
  Point this tunnel at the webhook port (4318, or the port after another person's server). The
  server's own port (4317) is a different door:
  it takes your devices, not webhooks. Putting it on the internet too is possible, behind an
  `https` address of its own, but Tailscale is the safer way to reach your bots from anywhere. See
  [Servers and addresses](/connections/#away-from-home).
</Aside>
