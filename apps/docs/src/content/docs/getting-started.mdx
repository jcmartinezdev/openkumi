This page takes you from a fresh install to a small team that works for you. It assumes the app is
installed ([Installing and updating](/install/)) and that one thing for the bots to run on is
signed in on this computer: Codex, Claude Code or Grok, or a local model
([What bots run on](/setup/providers/)).

## 1. Open OpenKumi

<Steps>

1. Open the app. It asks where your bots should run. Choose **Run them on this computer**, and
   say what you are called here: your bots are kept under that name on this computer. The name of
   your account on the computer is offered.

   (Choose **Connect to another computer** instead when your bots run on a computer of yours that
   already has OpenKumi; see [Other computers and your phone](/environments/).)

2. Open **Settings → Providers** and check that at least one shows as connected, such as "Codex:
   Signed in with ChatGPT". Each one that is not connected says why and what to do ("Run `codex
   login` in a terminal on this computer").

3. Close Settings. In the sidebar is **Kumi**, the bot that comes with OpenKumi.

</Steps>

## 2. Say hello to Kumi

Kumi is your **main assistant**: the first bot, the one for everyday questions and the first
stop for anything new. It is like the other bots in every way but three: it can make new bots, it
can set up automations for any bot, and it is the one to ask when you are not sure which bot fits.

Write to it as you would to a person. Some things to try:

- "What is on the front page of Hacker News? Show me the five most discussed as a table."
- "Compare the three cheapest flights from Lisbon to Berlin next Friday." It opens its own browser
  for this; a small live view floats over the chat while it works.
- "From now on, always answer in British English." It writes that into its own instructions
  (`AGENTS.md`), so it holds from then on.

A bot has **one conversation** with you, not a list of chats: whatever you say lands in the same
place, and it can look up anything said there, however long ago. While it works, you can keep
writing; your messages wait their turn in view, and **Send now** has it read one at once. See
[Talking with a bot](/using/conversation/).

## 3. Make a bot of your own

Make a bot for each kind of work you hand out, so each keeps its own memory, sign-ins and powers.
Either:

- press **+** in the sidebar, give it a name and say in a sentence or two what it is for, then
  **Create**; or
- ask Kumi: "Make me a bot called Travel that plans my trips, and have it find me a hotel in
  Porto for the 12th."

The new bot dresses itself (a mascot from its name and purpose), writes its own first
instructions from what it is for, and runs on what new bots run on (**Settings → General**). Change
any of that on its page: [Making and shaping a bot](/setup/bots/).

## 4. Give it a superpower

A superpower is something a bot can do beyond chatting and browsing: work in Notion, Linear or
Todoist, or know-how such as the weather or SEO. You switch each one on for one bot at a time.

<Steps>

1. Open **Settings → Superpowers** (or the bot's settings), pick one, and choose **Activate**.

2. If it works in an app, sign the bot in. The usual way is **With your browser**: the app's own
   sign-in page opens in your browser, and the bot gets an account of its own with it.

3. Ask the bot to use it: "What is assigned to me in Linear this week?"

</Steps>

See [Superpowers](/superpowers/) for the full list and the other ways to sign in.

## 5. Have it work without asking

An automation gives a bot a prompt by itself: at set times, when something changes, or when an app
calls. The quickest way to make one is to ask the bot:

> Every weekday at 8:00, look at my calendar and the weather and give me a short briefing.

It sets the automation up with its own tools; you see it under **Automations**, at the bottom of
the sidebar, where you can change, pause, run or delete it. When a run is done, the bot tells you
what came of it in its conversation. See [Automations](/automations/).

## 6. Reach it from anywhere

<Steps>

1. On this computer, open **Settings → Environments**, turn on **Let other computers connect**,
   and choose **Pair a computer**. It shows a code and a QR code.

2. On your laptop, open OpenKumi, choose **Connect to another computer**, and enter the address
   and the code. On the iPhone app, scan the QR code.

</Steps>

At home they connect directly; away from home, over [Tailscale](https://tailscale.com) when it is
on for both. See [Other computers and your phone](/environments/).
